\par
We observe that the $\theta$ that is related by the equation
\begin{equation}
    \sin(\theta) = \frac{\Delta x}{d}
\end{equation}
is equal to the $\theta$ represented by
\begin{equation}
    \tan(\theta) = \frac{y}{D}
\end{equation}
When the distance of both light rays' traveled paths is a multiple of the wavelength of the light, they engage in constructive interference.
We observe the fact that the path difference ($\Delta x$) for constructive waves is
\begin{equation}
    n\lambda
    \label{eq:path difference constructive}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the light and $n$ is an integer representing the nth bright fringe. where $n=0$ is the central maxima and negative values representing the nth bright fringe of the opposite direction. Hence we derive the equation.
\begin{align}
    \sin(\theta) &= \frac{\Delta x}{d} \\
    \sin(\theta) &= \frac{n\lambda}{d} \\
    \theta &= \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{n\lambda}{d}\right)
\end{align}
and since we know that the wavelength $\lambda$ of light is orders of magnitudes larger than slit spacing $d$, and hence the value in $\sin$ is close to zero, we can use the sin angle approximation $\lim_{\theta \to 0} \sin(\theta) = \theta$ to simplify to
\begin{equation}
    \theta = \frac{n\lambda}{d}
\end{equation}
relating $\theta$ to $d$ and $\lambda$.
This angle approximation for small values of $\theta$ also apply to $\tan(\theta)$ in the other representation of $\theta$
\begin{equation}
    \tan(\theta) = \frac{y}{D}
\end{equation}
and since we know that this is the same $\theta$ as the one used in $\sin(\theta)$ which is close to zero, we can simplify to %Adjust flow, how is "same theta" connected to "being approximable"
\begin{equation}
    \theta = \frac{y}{D}
\end{equation}
and putting them together
\begin{align}
    \frac{y}{D} &= \theta \\
    \frac{n \lambda}{d} &= \theta \\
    \frac{y}{D} &= \frac{n \lambda}{d} \\
    y  &= n\frac{D\lambda}{d} \\
\end{align}
To find the distance between fringes we need to find the distance of a fringe to the central maxima and distance of a consecutive fringe to the central maxima and find the the difference between their distances. % Diagram to show 
\par
We have defined the distance from the central maximum as 
\begin{itemize}
    \item the nth fringe as $y  = n\frac{D\lambda}{d}$
    \item the nth + 1 (adjacent) fringe as $y  = \left(n+1)\right)\frac{D\lambda}{d}$
\end{itemize}
finding the difference $\Delta y$ gives us the $s$ spacing between adjacent fringes
\begin{align}
    \Delta y = s&= \left(n+1\right) \frac{D \lambda}{d} - n \frac{D \lambda}{d} \\
    s &= \left(n+1-n\right) \frac{D \lambda}{d} \\
    s &= \frac{D \lambda}{d} 
\end{align}
and now we arrive at our destination.

The fringe distance is calculated by
\begin{equation}
    \text{Average Fringe Spacing} =  \frac{\Sigma\text{all fringe spacing data points}}{\text{number of data points}}% Capitalization?? how to do on equations?
\end{equation}

The distance is calculated by
\begin{equation}
    \text{Distance} = \text{initial distance} - \text{offset} + (\text{2 axis translation stage modifier})
\end{equation} %Capitalization?? how to do on equations?
Recall that every distance is based off of the origin point of the measuring tape, which is at 0cm. For instance the table is set to be [initial distance] away from the origin point of the measuring tape where D is one of the values for our independent variables mentioned earlier. However our 2 axis translation stage is closer to the table by a distance [offset] and must have that subtracted from the desired distance. Then we add the 2 axis translation stage modifier because recall that we have two systems responsible for controlling our independent variable, and this one is the one responsible for the "micro adjustments". % Read and make true to our definition of "origin"
The predicted values are calculated by plugging the values into the equation we derived in the beginning. Namely:
\begin{equation}
    s = \frac{\lambda d}{D}
\end{equation}
Where lambda is the wavelength of light of our laser (532nm) and d is the distance between slits 0.45mm
And hence we get the following data (some are duplicate due to the two system method we have chosen to measure) %explain how the system results in duplicates ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The difference is calculated with
\begin{equation}
    \text{Difference} = \left|\text{Measured Value} - \text{Predicted Value}\right|
\end{equation}
